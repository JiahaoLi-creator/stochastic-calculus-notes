Generators, Dynkin's Formula, and Martingale Residuals¶

1. The generator is the local conditional drift¶

For a time-homogeneous Markov process with semigroup $P_tf(x)=E_x[f(X_t)]$, the generator is

$$ Lf(x)=\lim_{h\downarrow 0}\frac{P_hf(x)-f(x)}{h}, $$

for functions $f$ in the domain $\mathcal D(L)$ for which this limit exists in the chosen function space. If

$$ dX_t=b(X_t)\,dt+\sigma(X_t)\,dB_t, $$

then Itô's formula gives, for sufficiently regular $f$,

$$ Lf(x)=b(x)f'(x)+\frac12\sigma(x)^2f''(x). $$

For the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process $dX_t=-\kappa X_tdt+\sigma dB_t$ and $f(x)=x^2$, $Lf(x)=\sigma^2-2\kappa x^2$.

Figure 1. The finite-horizon quotient $(P_hf-f)/h$ for $f(x)=x^2$ in an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck model, compared with its generator limit.

Interpretation. The quotient is a conditional expected rate of change over horizon $h$. As $h$ decreases, it approaches the infinitesimal drift $Lf(x)=\sigma^2-2\kappa x^2$ across the state grid.

2. Dynkin's residual is a martingale¶

Under the regularity and integrability conditions of Itô's formula,

$$ M_t^f=f(X_t)-f(X_0)-\int_0^t Lf(X_s)\,ds $$

is a local martingale. When it is square-integrable, its predictable quadratic variation is

$$ \langle M^f\rangle_t=\int_0^t \sigma(X_s)^2\bigl(f'(X_s)\bigr)^2ds. $$

For the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck example and $f(x)=x^2$, this is $\int_0^t4\sigma^2X_s^2ds$. Consequently, a numerical diagnostic should check the martingale mean and bracket identity, not assert that finite-mesh residual increments are independent normal noise. The notation $dB_t/dt$ for white noise is only distributional: Brownian paths have no ordinary time derivative.

Figure 2. Monte Carlo checks of the Dynkin residual mean and predictable-bracket identity for $f(x)=x^2$ in an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck model.

Interpretation. The mean residual remains close to zero, while its second moment follows the expected bracket. Small discrepancies come from time integration and Monte Carlo error; neither check requires finite-step residuals to be Gaussian or independent.

3. Stopping requires explicit conditions¶

Let $\tau$ be a bounded stopping time. If $f(X_{t\wedge\tau})$ and $Lf(X_t)\mathbf 1_{\{t<\tau\}}$ satisfy the required integrability conditions, optional stopping gives Dynkin's formula

$$ E_x[f(X_\tau)] =f(x)+E_x\!\left[\int_0^\tau Lf(X_s)\,ds\right]. $$

A standard localization is $\tau_a=\inf\{t\geq0:|X_t|\geq a\}\wedge T$, which is bounded by $T$. The next experiment checks the identity for several exit boundaries; it does not replace the integrability assumptions used to justify optional stopping.

Figure 3. A stopped Dynkin check for $f(x)=x^2$ and the proportion of Ornstein–Uhlenbeck paths exiting before $T=1.5$.

Interpretation. Across the bounded stopping rules, the mean stopped residual is consistent with zero at Monte Carlo scale. Wider boundaries reduce the exit probability; the theorem applies because the stopped process is treated together with explicit boundedness and integrability conditions.

4. Jump generators add a nonlocal term¶

For the compound Poisson process

$$ X_t=x+\sum_{i=1}^{N_t}Z_i, $$

with rate $\lambda$ and iid marks independent of $N$, the generator is

$$ Lf(x)=\lambda E[f(x+Z)-f(x)]. $$

For $f(x)=x^2$, mark mean $m_1=E[Z]$, and second moment $m_2=E[Z^2]$,

$$ Lf(x)=\lambda(2m_1x+m_2). $$

The Dynkin residual is again a martingale. Its bracket rate is the carré du champ

$$ \Gamma f(x)=L(f^2)(x)-2f(x)Lf(x) =\lambda E[(f(x+Z)-f(x))^2]. $$

Figure 4. Martingale-mean and bracket checks for $f(x)=x^2$ in a compound Poisson process with normal marks.

Interpretation. The jump generator accounts for the full finite change $f(x+Z)-f(x)$ rather than a local derivative alone. After subtracting its compensator, the residual has near-zero mean and a second moment governed by the nonlocal carré du champ.

5. Semigroups, backward equations, and martingale problems¶

The semigroup satisfies $P_{s+t}=P_sP_t$. For $f\in\mathcal D(L)$, differentiability in $t$ gives the backward Kolmogorov equation

$$ \partial_tu(t,x)=Lu(t,x), \qquad u(0,x)=f(x), \qquad u(t,x)=P_tf(x). $$

For the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process and $f(x)=x^2$,

$$ u(t,x)=e^{-2\kappa t}x^2+\frac{\sigma^2}{2\kappa}(1-e^{-2\kappa t}). $$

The martingale problem reverses the viewpoint: a probability law solves the $(L,\mathcal D(L))$ martingale problem if $M_t^f=f(X_t)-f(X_0)-\int_0^tLf(X_s)ds$ is a martingale for every $f\in\mathcal D(L)$. The operator identifies the law only when this martingale problem is well posed, meaning that existence and uniqueness in law have both been established.

Figure 5. The Ornstein–Uhlenbeck semigroup applied to $f(x)=x^2$ and a finite-difference check of the backward Kolmogorov equation.

Interpretation. The semigroup evolves the payoff over the time horizon, while the generator supplies its instantaneous evolution. The small numerical PDE residual links the conditional-expectation representation $u=P_tf$ to the equation $\partial_tu=Lu$.
